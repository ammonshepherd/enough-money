How to use Enough Money

1. Sign in and install

Go to enoughmoney.app/app and sign in with the email and password you were given. Accounts are by invitation: there is no sign-up page. Everyone in your household signs in to the same budget.

Put it on your home screen

  • iPhone or iPad (Safari): tap the Share button, then Add to Home Screen.
  • Android (Chrome): tap the ⋮ menu, then Add to Home screen or Install app.
  • Computer (Chrome or Edge): click the install icon at the right of the address bar.

It then opens full screen like any other app. When a new version is ready, a banner offers Refresh; the app never reloads in the middle of what you're doing.

Three tabs run along the bottom: Budget (your envelopes), Accounts (your transactions) and Settings. The ☰ button at the top right holds options for the page you're on.

2. Add your accounts

Open Accounts, tap ☰, and choose Add account. Pick a kind:

  • Checking (also savings or cash): enter the balance on the opening date. That money becomes Available to Assign.
  • Credit card: enter what you owe on the opening date, as a positive amount. The card gets its own payment envelope (see Credit cards).
  • Tracking: a loan, mortgage or investment you want to watch without it touching your budget.

The list groups your accounts into Checking, Credit cards and Tracking, with each balance. All accounts at the top shows every budget account's transactions for the month.

Tip: pick an opening date just before the first transaction you'll import or type, and use the balance from that day.

Accounts are closed rather than deleted, so their history stays. Open an account, tap its pencil, and choose Close account (it needs a $0.00 balance).

3. Set up envelopes

Envelopes live in groups, such as Bills and Everyday. Add, rename, reorder and delete groups in Settings → Envelope groups (drag the dots to reorder; tap a group to rename it or organise its envelopes).

On the Budget page, tap a group's + to add an envelope to it. Tap a group's name to fold it away.

Reading an envelope

  • The big number on the right is what's left to spend this month.
  • Planned: what you expect to spend. It's only a note to yourself; it changes nothing else. Tap it to type a new amount.
  • Assigned: money you've given the envelope this month.
  • Spent: what's gone out this month. It turns orange when it goes past Planned.
  • The Saved pill: money left over from earlier months (see Saved and Remaining).

Use the arrows in the purple band to change month. Envelopes you haven't used this month hide behind Show N envelopes not used this month at the bottom; any envelope that still holds money is always shown.

4. Assign your money

The green Available to Assign banner is money that doesn't have a job yet: your opening balances plus income, minus everything assigned. Your goal is to bring it to $0.00, when the banner disappears. If you assign more than you have, it turns red and says Over Assigned.

With the number pad

  1. Tap an envelope's Assigned amount.
  2. Type a new total, or tap + or − first to add to or take from what's there. The sum shows as you type.
  3. Tap Assign.

From the banner

Tap Available to Assign, start typing an envelope's name, pick it, and enter an amount. It's added to what the envelope already has.

Typing amounts: every amount field fills in cents first, like a cash register. Typing 1, 2, 3 shows 1.23. Type a period if you'd rather enter dollars (12.5 stays 12.50).

5. Add a transaction

Tap the round + at the bottom right of Budget or Accounts.

  1. Choose Outflow (money out) or Inflow.
  2. Enter the amount and who it was paid to or from.
  3. Type an envelope's name and pick it from the list. Each one shows what it has left.
  4. Check the account and date, add a memo if you like, and Save.

The envelope list also offers:

  • Income (to Available to Assign) for a paycheck or other money to budget.
  • Transfer (not spending or income) for money moving between your own accounts, including paying a credit card. One entry creates both sides, and links to the other account's bank row if it's already been imported.
  • Split across envelopes for one purchase that belongs to several envelopes.

To change or delete a transaction later, tap its payee in any list. Delete is at the bottom of the form.

6. When an envelope runs short

Envelopes never go negative. If a transaction would spend more than an envelope has left, the app stops and asks you to cover the shortfall before saving. It suggests, in this order:

  1. Release from this envelope's Saved, if it has some put aside.
  2. Move from another envelope that has money left.
  3. Assign more from Available to Assign.

Tap Cover and save to do both at once, or Back to change the transaction. In the picture, Fun Money has $60.00 left and $100.00 Saved, so an $85.00 purchase is covered by releasing $25.00 of its Saved.

7. Import from your bank

Enough Money never connects to your bank. Instead, download your transactions as a CSV file from your bank or card website, then:

  1. Open the account on Accounts.
  2. Tap Import CSV and choose the file.
  3. The first time, check the columns it found (date, description, amount). Card files that show spending as positive have a box to flip signs. The setup is remembered for that account.
  4. Read the preview, then tap Import.

The preview tells you exactly what will happen:

  • rows already imported are skipped as duplicates;
  • rows dated before the account's opening date are skipped;
  • rows it can't read are listed with the reason;
  • a row matching a transaction you typed in yourself (same amount, within 10 days) is matched to it instead of added twice. Untick Match to import it as new;
  • inflows your income rules recognise are marked as Income.

8. Review what came in

Imported transactions arrive Uncategorized, with an amber label, and wait in the To review card on All accounts and on each account. Until you decide what they are, they don't count toward anything. The Budget page tells you how many are waiting.

  • Money out: tap it and give it an envelope (or make it a Transfer).
  • Money in: tap Mark as Income for a paycheck, or give it an envelope if it's a refund.
Income rules (in Settings) mark paychecks for you. Add a word from the description, such as "salary", and every matching inflow is marked as Income when it's imported. You can also apply rules to what's already waiting.

Refunds from a credit card, cashback and statement credits are handled for you: they simply lower what the card owes and never need reviewing.

9. Saved and Remaining

Tap an envelope's name to open it. You'll see what's Remaining this month and how it adds up, how you're doing against Planned, its Planned and Assigned amounts, and this month's transactions.

How Saved grows

Whatever is left in an envelope at the end of a month moves into its Saved reserve, and Saved keeps adding up month after month. It's still the envelope's money, but it isn't spendable until you say so. The envelope's total is Saved plus Remaining.

Using Saved

In the purple Saved panel, tap Move some into Remaining… and enter an amount. That money is now spendable this month. Releasing Saved doesn't change Available to Assign: the money never leaves the envelope.

Why a reserve? It turns "I have money left over" into a choice rather than a temptation. Saving up for a holiday or a car repair happens on its own, envelope by envelope.

10. Move money between envelopes

Plans change. Tap the amount left in an envelope to open Move money:

  1. Choose where it goes: another envelope, or back to Available to Assign.
  2. Enter how much, and tap Move.

The amount comes off this envelope's Assigned and goes onto the other's. You can move up to what's left (and no more than was assigned this month), in this month or a later one. Money from a card's payment envelope can't be moved this way.

11. Credit cards

Each card has a payment envelope, named after the card, in the Credit card payments group. You don't put purchases in it; it fills itself:

  • A card purchase in an envelope moves that money from the envelope into the card's payment envelope. The spending shows in the envelope you chose; the cash to pay for it is now waiting to pay the card.
  • Paying the card (a Transfer from checking) takes the money back out of the payment envelope.
  • Refunds and statement credits just lower what you owe.

The payment envelope shows two numbers:

  • Available to pay: money set aside for the card.
  • Remaining debt: what you owe beyond that, often debt from before you started. Assign money to the payment envelope to pay it down.

The red Credit card debt banner shows what all your cards owe today, on every page, until it reaches $0.00.

12. Search and reconcile

Open an account to see its balance, this month's money in and out, its opening balance and what's reconciled, then every transaction under dated headings. Tap a day's heading to fold it.

Search

Type in Search or pick an envelope to find a payee, memo or amount, or pick an envelope or a kind (Uncategorized, Income, Transfer, Card credit, Not reconciled) from the list. ☰ sorts by date, name, amount, envelope or income and expense.

Reconcile

Tick the rows that match your bank statement, then tap Mark reconciled. Their bank icon turns green, and the account's Reconciled figure shows the balance of everything you've checked.

13. Start a new month

Go to the next month with the arrow in the purple band. A month whose Planned amounts are all $0.00 offers to start your plan for you:

  • copy last month's Planned, or
  • copy last month's Spent (handy once you know your habits), or
  • upload a plan as a CSV file (also in Settings → Import envelope plan).

Then assign this month's money. Last month's leftovers are already in each envelope's Saved.

14. Settings and backup

Settings is where you:

  • organise envelope groups;
  • add or remove income rules;
  • import an envelope plan;
  • export a backup: your whole household in one file. Keep a copy from time to time. A backup can be restored into an empty household;
  • see which version you're running (About Enough Money).

Sign out from the ☰ menu on Settings.

15. On a computer

On a wide screen, a sidebar replaces the bottom tabs and lists every account with its balance. The Budget page shows each envelope on one line, and choosing an envelope or a transaction opens it in a panel on the right instead of a sheet. Assigned is typed in place, and accepts sums like +50.00. Both the sidebar and the panel can be collapsed.
